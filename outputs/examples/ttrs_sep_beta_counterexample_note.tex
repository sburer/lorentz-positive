\title{A Two-Dimensional Counterexample for the SEP and Beta TTRS Relaxations}
\author{Computational note}
\date{September 13, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\noindent
This note records the small counterexample referenced at the end of Section 4.2
of the current paper draft.  It is written in the notation of the paper:
\(x\in\Rbb^n\) is the original TTRS variable, \(A=\Diag(a)\) is diagonal
positive definite, and the second ball constraint is written through
\(y=Ax+b\).

\section{Instance}

Consider
\begin{equation}
    \begin{array}{ll}
    \min & x\tran Qx+c\tran x\\
    {\rm s.t.} & \norm{x}\leq 1,\\
                & \norm{Ax+b}\leq 1,
    \end{array}
    \label{eq:ttrs-counterexample}
\end{equation}
where \(n=2\),
\[
    a=
    \begin{bmatrix}
    4/3\\[2pt]
    13/12
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    b=
    \begin{bmatrix}
    11/20\\[2pt]
    -1/20
    \end{bmatrix},
\]
and
\[
Q=
\begin{bmatrix}
-24/16 & -1/16\\[2pt]
-1/16 & -13/16
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
c=
\begin{bmatrix}
-1\\[2pt]
0
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The smallest eigenvalue of \(Q\) is approximately \(-1.505635621484\), so the
objective is nonconvex.

\section{SEP Relaxation}

The Full SEP relaxation uses the Shor moment matrix
\[
    H=
    \begin{bmatrix}
    1 & x\tran\\
    x & X
    \end{bmatrix}\succeq0
\]
together with the two trace inequalities
\[
    {\rm tr}(X)\leq1,\qquad
    {\rm tr}(A^2X)+2b\tran Ax+\norm{b}^2\leq1.
\]
It also introduces
\[
    Z=
    \begin{bmatrix}
    1 & x\tran\\
    y & V
    \end{bmatrix}
    \in\SEP(n+1,n+1),
\]
where \(y=Ax+b\), and imposes the affine consistency equations
\[
    Z_{0j}=x_j,\qquad
    Z_{i0}=a_ix_i+b_i,\qquad
    Z_{ij}=a_iX_{ij}+b_ix_j.
\]
The objective is \(\mprod{Q}{X}+c\tran x\).  On this instance, the Full SEP
relaxation gives
\[
    z_{\rm SEP}=-0.632844508289.
\]

\section{Shifted Beta Relaxation}

The shifted beta relaxation introduces variables \(\beta_i\) representing
\(x_i^2\).  With \(w=(1,x,\beta)\), the two ball constraints become
\[
    1-\sum_i\beta_i\geq0,
\]
and
\[
    1-\norm{b}^2-2\sum_i a_ib_ix_i-\sum_i a_i^2\beta_i\geq0.
\]
The inequalities \(\beta_i\geq x_i^2\) are imposed with rotated second-order
cones, and the beta SDP then includes the Shor, RLT, SOC-RLT, and Kronecker
constraints in the same spirit as the beta relaxation used for the concentric
TTRS instances.  For this shifted instance, the relaxation gives
\[
    z_{\beta}=-0.550899217259.
\]

\section{True Optimum}

The true optimum was certified by enumerating the one-dimensional boundary arcs
of the feasible intersection.  The value is
\[
    z^\star=-0.546797627007,
\]
attained at approximately
\[
    x^\star =
    \begin{bmatrix}
    0.185929524305185\\[2pt]
    0.602567865182517
    \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Thus both relaxations are strict lower bounds:
\[
    z^\star-z_{\rm SEP}=0.086046881282,\qquad
    z^\star-z_{\beta}=0.004101590252.
\]

\section{Diagnostic Values}

At the Full SEP optimum, the first-column point is well inside both ball
constraints:
\[
    \norm{x}^2=0.139541363013,\qquad
    \norm{Ax+b}^2=0.004339906310.
\]
The trace quantities behave differently:
\[
    {\rm tr}(X)=0.808104046269,\qquad
    {\rm tr}(A^2X)+2b\tran Ax+\norm{b}^2=1.000000000282.
\]
The rank-one residual of the Shor moment matrix is approximately
\[
    0.498013951456.
\]
This is consistent with the interpretation that the missing ingredient is
synchronization between the lifted quadratic representation and the product
cone representation.

\section{Reproduction}

The script
\[
    \texttt{paper-code/verify\_ttrs\_sep\_beta\_counterexample.py}
\]
re-solves the Shor relaxation, the Full SEP relaxation, and the shifted beta
relaxation, and then certifies the true value by boundary enumeration.  It is a
named reproducibility check rather than part of the random computational panels.
